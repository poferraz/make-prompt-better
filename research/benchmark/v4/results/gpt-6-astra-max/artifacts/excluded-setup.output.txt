Yes. The function mixes percentage units: `+= 10` assumes whole percentages, but the multiplication treats the discount as a fraction.

For example, `calculate_discount(100, 20, False)` returns `0` instead of `80`.

Assuming `20` means 20% and membership adds 10 percentage points:

```python
def calculate_discount(price, discount_percent, is_member):
    if is_member:
        discount_percent += 10
    final_price = price - (price * discount_percent / 100)
    return max(0, final_price)
```

You may also want input validation: negative discounts currently increase the price, and negative prices silently return zero for ordinary discount values.
